Documentation 03 - Site architecture

Site architecture

The sitemap, what each page must contain, the commission enquiry flow, and the photography and copy that have to exist before any of it can launch. Structure is independent of the visual direction - this document holds whichever of the three is chosen.

Sitemap

Seven pages. Both reference studios run five to six; the additions are the queue page and the terms page, which are the two places where the brief identified a real competitive gap.

/                    Home
/work/               Portfolio - grid of completed suits
/work/<suit>/        Individual suit - full photo set, build notes
/prices/             Commission tiers, add-ons, payment terms
/commissions/        How to commission, current status, enquiry form
/queue/              Live build queue
/terms/              Terms of service
/about/              The maker, the studio, contact
/thanks/             Enquiry received confirmation
Deliberately absent No blog, no shop, no login, no newsletter. Each would add a build step, a backend, or a maintenance burden the studio has to sustain. A dormant blog with three posts from 2026 actively damages credibility. Social feeds already do the "recent activity" job; link to them instead.

Page specifications

Home

One job: convince a stranger this maker is good, in under ten seconds, then send them to the portfolio. It is not a summary of the whole site.

BlockContainsNotes
HeroWordmark, one-line positioning, one strong photograph, two buttons The photograph is the argument. Everything else is caption.
Status strip"Slots open" / "Waitlist only" / "Closed", with the next opening date Single most-read element for returning visitors. Must be trivially editable - one line in one file.
Selected workFour to six suits, linking through to the portfolio Best work only. A weak sixth entry costs more than an empty slot.
Tier summaryFour tiers with starting prices, linking to prices Sets expectation early. Filters out enquiries that were never going to convert.
ProcessThree or four steps, foam to finish Answers "what happens after I pay", which is the real anxiety at these amounts.
FooterSocial links, email, terms, copyrightSame on every page.

Portfolio

A grid of completed suits, newest first, each opening a detail page. Filters by tier and by style only once there are more than about twenty suits - before that they are friction with no benefit.

Each detail page carries: six to twelve photographs, the character name, the owner's credit and social handle with permission, the tier, the build year, and two or three sentences of build notes. Those notes are the most persuasive copy on the site, because they are the only place the maker's competence is visible in words rather than pictures.

Prices

Ranges, not single figures, with a plain statement of what moves a price within its range - colour count, character complexity, and extras. Both reference studios publish ranges; matching them is table stakes.

Also needed on this page: add-ons (extra fans, LED eyes, magnetic parts, wings), payment plan terms, deposit amount and whether it is refundable, and shipping.

Commissions

The conversion page, and the only page with a form. Three sections in order: current status, what the maker needs from you, and the enquiry form.

"What the maker needs" should be specific and demanding - a reference sheet, measurements or a DTD, a tier, a budget, and a deadline if any. Asking properly here saves the six-message exchange that Heckhoundz avoids by refusing quotes entirely. Neither reference site publishes DTD instructions; including them is a small, genuinely useful differentiator.

Queue

The differentiating page. A simple table: position, character, tier, current stage, and a month-level estimate. First names or handles only, with permission.

1   Vesper    Plush digi fullsuit    Fur - body        est. Sept 2026
2   Koa       Mini partial           Foam - head       est. Oct 2026
3   (open)    -                      -                 -

It costs one hand-edit a fortnight and does more for trust than any amount of copy, because it is the only page on any competitor site that answers "am I being forgotten". It also reduces "where is my suit" messages, which is the maker's own time back.

Terms of service

Neither reference studio publishes one. At $3,000 to $12,000 that is a real risk for both parties. Must cover: deposit and refundability, payment schedule, what happens if the client cancels, what happens if the maker cannot complete, turnaround estimates and what invalidates them, revision and approval points, shipping and insurance, repair and cleaning policy, photography and publicity rights, and character copyright - who owns the design and what the maker may show.

Not legal advice This is a content specification, not a contract. The finished terms should be reviewed by somebody qualified in the studio's jurisdiction before publication.

About

The maker, honestly. Years of experience, what drew them to the craft, where they work, what they specialise in, and a photograph of the person or their workspace. Both reference studios lead with personality and it works - "meet the artist" on Fur Me Creations, the "Heckhoundz family" framing on Heckhoundz. Contact details and social links live here too.

Commission enquiry flow

Portfolio ──► Prices ──► Commissions ──► Enquiry form ──► /thanks/
                                              │
                                              ▼
                                   email to the studio
                                              │
                                              ▼
                              maker replies with quote + slot
                                              │
                                              ▼
                            deposit ──► queue entry ──► build

The site's responsibility ends at the enquiry. Everything after it happens over email, which is correct - it needs a human, and building anything more would mean a backend the studio has to run.

Form handling on a static site

A static site cannot process a form itself. Three options, in preference order:

OptionHowTrade-off
Host form handler Netlify Forms or Cloudflare Workers - the host receives the POST and emails the studio Recommended. No third-party origin, no JavaScript needed, spam filtering included. Ties the form to the host, which is a small cost.
Third-party serviceFormspree, Basin Host-portable, but adds an external origin that the content security policy must permit and routes enquiries through another company.
Mailto linkPlain email link, no form Zero infrastructure and always works, but loses the structured fields, which were the entire point.

Whichever is chosen, the form must have: a honeypot field, a server-side length cap on every field, no file uploads (ask for links to reference art instead - accepting uploads on a brochure site is unnecessary risk), and a rate limit at the host. It collects an email address and a character description, so a short privacy line stating what is stored and for how long belongs in the footer.

The real critical path Photography, not code. The site can be built in a week; a proper shoot of existing suits takes planning, a location, and possibly the owners bringing their suits in. Start arranging it now - it will finish last regardless of when the build starts.

Technical notes

  • URLs - directory style with trailing slashes (/work/vesper/), so nothing breaks if a build step is ever introduced.
  • Images - AVIF with a JPEG fallback via <picture>, several widths in srcset, explicit width and height on every image to prevent layout shift, loading="lazy" on everything below the fold.
  • No client-side routing - real pages, real URLs. Works without JavaScript, which matters for sharing and for search.
  • JavaScript budget - the gallery lightbox and the mobile navigation toggle. Nothing else should need it.
  • Structured data - LocalBusiness on About, Product on tier entries. Cheap, and it improves how the studio surfaces in search.
  • Accessibility - alt text on every suit photograph describing the character, keyboard- operable lightbox and navigation, visible focus rings, and contrast checked against the chosen direction's palette.
